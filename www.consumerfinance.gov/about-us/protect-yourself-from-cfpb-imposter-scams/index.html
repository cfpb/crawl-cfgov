Protect yourself from CFPB imposter scams

Is someone who claims to work for the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) contacting you? Scammers sometimes use our name or the names of real employees to try to get your money or personal information.

The CFPB will never:

  • ask you for sensitive information or to pay money
  • ask you to pay an upfront fee or taxes
  • tell you that you have won a lottery, sweepstakes, or class-action lawsuit
  • ask you for personal or sensitive information before you can cash a check we have issued

If you receive a call or message from someone claiming to be from the CFPB, stop and verify it.

Stop. Do not send money or share personal or financial information in response to unexpected contact.

Verify. Call the CFPB directly at (855) 411-2372, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m., Eastern time, except federal holidays. Use this number rather than one provided by the person who contacted you.

Report. Report suspected scams to the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) at ReportFraud.ftc.gov .

Recognize the warning signs

Scammers may contact you by phone, video call, email, text message, mail, social media, or messaging app. They may use a government employee’s name or official-looking documents to make their story seem believable.

The CFPB will never ask you to pay upfront fees or taxes to receive money, or ask for personal or sensitive information before you can cash a check we have issued.

Watch for someone who:

  • Promises money if you pay first. They say you are entitled to a lawsuit payment, prize, or other money, but must pay taxes or fees before receiving it. They may keep finding reasons to demand additional payments.
  • Pressures you to act immediately. They want you to pay or share information before you have time to check their story.
  • Tells you how to pay. They direct you to send money by gift card, cryptocurrency, wire transfer, or payment app.
  • Requests sensitive information unexpectedly. They ask for information such as your Social Security number, bank account details, or account password.

A familiar name, government logo, or official-looking phone number does not prove that a contact is legitimate.

Check whether a person contacting you is legitimate

If you are unsure if a person contacting you is really from the CFPB, end the conversation and call us directly at (855) 411-2372, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m., Eastern time, except federal holidays.

Don’t rely on caller ID. Scammers can make a call appear to come from a government agency. Don’t click opening links and attachments in unexpected messages, and do not use contact information supplied by the person you are trying to verify.

If our call center is closed, pause before taking action. You can review official information on consumerfinance.gov while you wait to speak with us. Do not let someone pressure you into paying or sharing information before you can verify their claim.

If someone contacts you about the status of a complaint you submitted

Go directly to consumerfinance.gov/complaint to access the complaint process and check your complaint’s status. If you’re unsure whether a message about your complaint is legitimate, call us directly at (855) 411-2372, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m., Eastern time, except federal holidays.

If someone contacts you about a payment or check

Some CFPB enforcement actions result in payments to consumers. Those payments could come from the CFPB, a company, or a payment administrator.

Visit our payments by case page to look for case information and find contact information for payment questions. Use the contact information you find through our website to check a payment claim.

Consumer advisories about CFPB imposter scams

Read about specific scams reported to the CFPB, what scammers said, and how to protect yourself.

A scam does not have to appear on this page to be suspicious. Scammers may change their names, contact information, and stories.

Get help after a suspected scam

If you think you sent money or information to a scammer, take action right away. Follow the FTC’s steps to take after a scam , including guidance for checking your device and protecting your accounts. Acting quickly can help protect your accounts and limit further harm.

Report a suspected scam

To report a scam: Visit the Federal Trade Commission at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. You can report an attempted scam even if you did not send money.

To verify someone claiming to represent the CFPB: Call (855) 411-2372, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. Eastern time, except federal holidays.
